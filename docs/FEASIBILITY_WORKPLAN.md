# Feasibility Workplan and Evidence Protocol

## Purpose
Determine whether the founder's land can support a lawful, technically deliverable, commercially viable project. The 40-story concept, 60-floor alternative, and underground levels are scenarios to test—not entitlements, engineering instructions, or promises.

## Workstream 1 — Establish the site facts
Collect privately and verify:
- Country, city, municipality, planning authority, address, and parcel/cadastral reference.
- Gross parcel area, dimensions, boundaries, road frontage, access rights, easements, rights-of-way, and neighboring uses.
- Current title holder, title type, liens, mortgages, litigation, restrictions, co-owners, inheritance/consent issues, and transfer limitations.
- Existing buildings, occupants, leases, utilities, access, contamination history, and known disputes.
- Topographic survey, available geotechnical records, flood/groundwater information, and environmental constraints.

**Output:** verified site fact sheet, redacted public version, title diligence issues list, and source register.

## Workstream 2 — Planning and legal entitlement
Obtain current written information from the relevant authority and qualified local planning counsel:
- Current zoning/land-use designation and permitted uses.
- Maximum height, floor-area ratio / plot ratio, site coverage, setbacks, daylight / shadow constraints, density, parking, loading, access, public realm, and open-space requirements.
- Heritage, airport/aviation, view-corridor, environmental, seismic, coastal/flood, fire access, and infrastructure overlays where applicable.
- Whether a lawful rezoning, plan amendment, variance, special permit, density bonus, or public hearing route exists; the process, criteria, fees, time, appeal rights, and probability must not be represented as guaranteed.
- Development charges, impact fees, utility contributions, affordable-housing or public-benefit obligations, and any land dedication requirements.
- Any ownership, foreign investment, sanctions, land-use, or financing restrictions relevant to the jurisdiction.

**Output:** planning constraints memorandum with primary-source citations, written authority responses, assumptions, open legal questions, and a clear distinction between existing rights and discretionary approvals.

## Workstream 3 — Technical site screening
Commission appropriately licensed professionals, in stages:
- Boundary/topographic survey.
- Desktop geotechnical and environmental review, followed by field work only when justified.
- Groundwater, excavation support, neighboring-foundation effects, contamination, flood and drainage risks.
- Utility capacity and upgrade requirements.
- Road/transport access, emergency access, construction logistics, crane/laydown constraints.
- Wind, seismic, fire/life safety, accessibility, evacuation, smoke control, lift/vertical circulation, resilience, energy, and water strategy.
- Basement feasibility: excavation depth, groundwater control, retaining systems, spoil removal, adjacent assets, utilities, legal easements, construction impacts, and whole-life cost.

No agent should create construction-ready structural, fire, geotechnical, or MEP designs. Those must be produced and signed by qualified local professionals.

## Workstream 4 — Market and program
Research independently:
- Competing projects and pipeline; use comparable properties with location, date, area, use, occupancy, rent or sale evidence, and source quality.
- Demand by use: residential, office, hotel, retail, medical, education, technology, or other locally lawful uses.
- Achievable rents/prices, incentives, vacancy, absorption, tenant size, lease term, fit-out obligations, and operating costs.
- Mobility, amenities, employment, transit, and neighborhood context.
- Tenant pre-lease interest: distinguish casual feedback, non-binding expressions of interest, heads of terms, and executed leases.

**Output:** market report with dated comparables, source trail, downside case, and recommended program mix. Do not assume that an AI company or any named corporation wants space.

## Workstream 5 — Concept and alternatives
Compare at least:
1. 40-story concept.
2. 60-floor aspiration, only if planning capacity and technical evidence justify testing it.
3. Mid-rise / smaller tower.
4. Low-rise or phased development.
5. Joint venture with established developer.
6. Sale, ground lease, or long-term lease of the land.
7. Hold / no-build alternative.

For each: legal pathway, gross and net areas, usable efficiency assumptions, program, parking/loading, schedule, cost methodology, revenue assumptions, funding gap, key risks, and residual value. Floor counts are not substitutes for permitted floor area or a tested program.

## Workstream 6 — Cost and schedule
Use a local quantity surveyor / cost consultant and relevant engineers. Separate:
- Land value and acquisition/transfer costs (avoid double-counting if contributed).
- Professional fees, surveys, due diligence, planning and permit fees.
- Enabling works, demolition, remediation, utilities, roads, public realm.
- Substructure / basements and superstructure.
- Facade, vertical transport, MEP, fire/life safety, fit-out, and specialist systems.
- Taxes, duties, insurance, financing fees and interest during construction.
- Contingency, escalation, owner costs, marketing/leasing, and operating start-up.
- Phasing, temporary works, logistics, commissioning, and handover.

Every estimate must state date, currency, measurement basis, scope, exclusions, design maturity, source, and contingency. Do not extrapolate a cost per square metre from an unrelated city or building without explicit adjustments.

## Workstream 7 — Financing and delivery
Test the land-contribution JV, strategic developer partnership, equity, construction debt, phased delivery, presales/pre-leasing (only where lawful), ground lease, and eligible incentives. Document prerequisites, security, recourse, dilution, control, conditions precedent, and the equity gap. Do not assume lenders will accept land as sufficient collateral or equity.

## Evidence register format
For every material input record:
- ID and description.
- Value / statement and units.
- Source organization, exact URL or document, publication / effective date, access date.
- Source type (official, professional, market transaction, broker, interview, assumption).
- Geography and relevance.
- Confidence (high / medium / low) with reason.
- Status (unverified / checked / independently confirmed / superseded).
- Owner, next verification action, and affected model cells.

## Decision gates
- Gate 0: founder confirms the project brief and supplies safe, non-sensitive site facts.
- Gate 1: title and site identity have no obvious fatal issue; legal counsel identifies the title diligence path.
- Gate 2: planning review identifies a credible, lawful entitlement pathway.
- Gate 3: technical screening identifies no unmanageable site constraint; basement remains optional.
- Gate 4: market evidence supports a plausible use and program.
- Gate 5: independent cost plan and financial model show a credible residual value and funding route.
- Gate 6: experienced delivery partner, governance, capital plan, and professional team are credible.
- Gate 7: required approvals, signed agreements, financing, insurance, and construction safety arrangements are in place before construction.

A failed gate triggers redesign, scale reduction, pause, or no-go—not concealment or rule avoidance.
