# Investor and Development-Partner Readiness

## Current status
The project is at concept / information-gathering stage. It is not currently represented as investment-ready. There are no verified land details in this repository and no confirmed approvals, valuation, capital, partner, tenant, or financing commitments.

## Minimum evidence room
Maintain a private, access-controlled diligence folder. Do not upload unredacted title, identity, bank or personal financial documents to this public repository.

### A. Land and ownership
- Official title / registry extract and legal opinion.
- Cadastral plan, measured survey, boundary and access rights.
- Liens, easements, mortgages, restrictions, litigation, co-owner consents.
- Independent valuation with date, method, assumptions and limitations.
- Existing leases, occupation, rights, disputes and vacant-possession status.

### B. Planning and approvals
- Current zoning/land-use designation and authoritative planning extracts.
- Permitted use, height, density/FAR, coverage, setbacks, parking, access.
- Written pre-application feedback where available.
- Entitlement route, hearings, public process, fees, obligations and appeal risks.
- Full approvals tracker and conditions; no approval is assumed.

### C. Site and technical
- Topographic and boundary survey.
- Geotechnical, groundwater, flood, environmental and contamination reports.
- Utilities and transport capacity.
- Preliminary fire/life safety, emergency access, accessibility and resilience strategy.
- Basement/excavation constraints and neighboring asset risks.
- Independent professional qualifications and scope limitations.

### D. Market and commercial
- Local market study, dated comparables and demand analysis.
- Proposed use mix and area schedule.
- Rent/sale assumptions, vacancy, absorption and incentives.
- Tenant conversations, carefully classified by status.
- Operating, maintenance, leasing, tax and lifecycle costs.

### E. Cost, schedule, delivery
- Independent cost plan with scope, date, currency, exclusions and contingency.
- Development program and critical path.
- Procurement and delivery strategy.
- Construction risk, insurance, safety and quality plans.
- Professional services proposals and qualifications.

### F. Corporate and financing
- Proposed entity / SPV chart reviewed by local counsel.
- Beneficial ownership, governance, reserved matters and conflicts.
- Land contribution / lease / JV term sheet, subject to independent advice.
- Capital stack and sources-and-uses schedule.
- Debt terms, security, recourse, covenants and conditions precedent.
- Equity terms, dilution, preferred return, waterfall and exit.
- Tax, accounting, regulatory and securities-law review.

## Investor presentation structure
1. Project thesis and current stage.
2. Location and verified site facts.
3. Existing planning position and entitlement uncertainty.
4. Concept alternatives (40 floors, 60-floor option, smaller/phased case).
5. Market evidence and target users.
6. Technical constraints and underground optionality.
7. Development budget, assumptions, contingency and schedule.
8. Capital requirement, equity gap, proposed partnership structure.
9. Sensitivity and downside analysis.
10. Team, governance, risks and mitigations.
11. Diligence materials and open questions.
12. Specific request (e.g., paid feasibility mandate, partner capability discussion, or conditional JV exploration).

## Required disclosures
- Floor count is aspirational until entitlement and technical review.
- All budgets and returns are estimates with clearly stated scope and limitations.
- Planning permissions and approvals are not guaranteed.
- Financing and investor demand are not committed.
- A land contribution is subject to title, independent valuation, legal and tax review.
- AI or technology tenants are not confirmed.
- Past project experience must be attributed only to the professionals or partners who actually performed it.
- No offer of securities or investment solicitation should be made until counsel confirms applicable requirements and approves materials.

## Version control and confidentiality
Keep public materials high-level and redacted. Maintain a separate private evidence room with a document index, access log, version date and responsible owner. Use watermarks where appropriate, but do not treat watermarks as a substitute for access control.
