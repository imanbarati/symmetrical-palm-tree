# Funding and Development-Partner Strategy

## Purpose
Identify realistic, lawful ways to test whether the founder's land can attract experienced development capability and capital. The strategy does not guarantee fundraising and does not assume the founder can finance the project personally.

## Starting premise
The founder reports owning land and having very limited cash. First verify whether the land is legally owned, transferable or leaseable, unencumbered or capable of being dealt with, independently valued, and suitable for the proposed development. Do not pledge, transfer, mortgage, option, or contribute the land before independent legal advice.

## Potential routes (not commitments)
### 1. Land-contribution joint venture
The founder may contribute land, while a developer/investor contributes some combination of development expertise, project management, equity, guarantees, and capital. Key negotiations:
- Independent land valuation and agreed valuation date.
- Whether the land is transferred, leased, optioned, or held outside the SPV.
- Ownership split, preferred return, profit waterfall, and distribution timing.
- Who funds surveys, approvals, design and early-stage costs.
- Who bears cost overruns, delays, remediation, financing and guarantee obligations.
- Control, reserved matters, reporting, capital calls, dilution, deadlock, exit, default, and dispute resolution.
- Conditions precedent, including title diligence, planning, funding, approvals, and partner track record.
- Protection against losing the land through poorly understood security or default clauses.

A land-for-equity arrangement can still expose the owner to substantial risk and may not be accepted by investors. Obtain independent valuation and counsel.

### 2. Strategic development partner
Approach established developers with relevant local high-rise experience and completed projects. A credible partner may contribute feasibility resources and delivery capability. Screen audited/project evidence, completed assets, litigation, financial capacity, references, safety record, and actual decision-makers.

### 3. Equity investors
Potential classes include private real-estate investors, family offices, institutional investors, funds, and strategic corporate investors. They generally require verified title, a credible planning pathway, market evidence, independent cost estimates, governance, risk disclosure, and a professional team. Securities offering, solicitation, advertising, foreign-investment, and investor-qualification rules vary by jurisdiction. Do not publicly promise returns or accept money without local legal advice.

### 4. Construction or development debt
Usually later-stage, not a substitute for early feasibility funding. Lenders may require sponsor equity, experienced developer guarantees, permits, independent quantity-surveyor reports, pre-leases/pre-sales, valuation, insurance, contingency, and covenants. The land may not count as sufficient equity or acceptable collateral.

### 5. Ground lease or land lease
Retain title while granting a long-term lease to an experienced developer, where lawful and commercially appropriate. Terms must cover rent, development obligations, milestones, assignment, lender rights, reversion, defaults, maintenance, taxes, insurance, and termination.

### 6. Sale, option, or conditional sale
A full or partial sale, conditional option, or staged transaction may unlock value or reduce exposure. Compare net proceeds, tax, control, timing, conditions and downside risks. Never sign an option or exclusivity agreement without counsel.

### 7. Smaller / phased development
A lower-capital phase, smaller permitted building, or enabling/serviced parcel strategy may be more financeable than a 40- or 60-floor tower. Compare whole-project value, interface risks, temporary works, and future phase dependencies.

### 8. Public incentives / development programs
Investigate only programs for which the project is genuinely eligible (for example, affordable housing, regeneration, transit-oriented development, energy efficiency, or employment). Treat support as zero until formal eligibility and award are confirmed. Do not plan around discretionary grants as guaranteed money.

### 9. Tenant pre-commitments
Credible pre-leasing may support finance in some markets, but tenant demand must be independently evidenced and legal presale/prelease conditions met. A non-binding conversation is not a lease or funding commitment.

## AI and technology companies
Treat AI firms as potential future tenants, technology vendors, or service partners—not assumed financiers. Any lease, rent-free period, barter, service credits, equity, or in-kind consideration would require:
- A viable, permitted and completed space.
- Independent market valuation of both sides of any exchange.
- Defined deliverables, service levels, term, taxes, payment timing and remedies.
- Intellectual property, data, privacy, cyber-security, confidentiality and liability terms.
- Corporate authority, conflict review, legal and accounting/tax review.
- Written, approved agreements. No company is committed unless it has signed.

Do not promise rent to a provider “in kind” without a defensible valuation and a legal agreement. It cannot replace the initial equity or construction funding unless a funder explicitly accepts it.

## Partner selection scorecard
Score each candidate only from verifiable evidence:
- Relevant projects completed (not just announced).
- High-rise and underground experience where relevant.
- Financial capacity and proof of funds where appropriately verified.
- Litigation, insolvency, regulatory and sanctions screening.
- Safety, quality, environmental and delivery record.
- Local permits / authority experience.
- References from landowners, lenders, consultants and tenants.
- Proposed commercial terms, governance, and conflicts.
- Willingness to fund early due diligence and bear a fair share of risk.
- Ability to provide transparent reporting and independent audits.

## Recommended sequence
1. Verify title and planning basics.
2. Produce a two-page, non-confidential site teaser with no sensitive title documents.
3. Obtain a local broker / development advisor's input on likely partner universe.
4. Build a shortlist based on completed comparable projects.
5. Use a mutual NDA only where justified and counsel-reviewed; do not use an NDA to conceal material project risks.
6. Request capability statements, project references, indicative structure and early-stage funding proposal.
7. Compare offers with independent counsel, valuer and advisor.
8. Sign no exclusivity, option, land transfer, guarantee, security, or JV agreement before due diligence and independent advice.

## Anti-fraud controls
- Never pay upfront “release fees,” guaranteed-investment fees, personal account transfers, crypto deposits, or unexplained commissions to obtain funding.
- Independently verify lender, fund, broker, investor and advisor identities via official registers and known contact details.
- Do not share passport scans, title originals, bank credentials, signing keys, or sensitive personal financial information in a public repository.
- Do not fabricate investor interest, term sheets, LOIs, permits, pre-leases, valuations or construction costs.
- Record introducer fees, conflicts and commissions in writing and have counsel review them.
